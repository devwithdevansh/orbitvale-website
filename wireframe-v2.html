01 — The core idea

The page is not a stack of sections. It is an orbit — one camera, one object, and every letter of ORBITVALE is a place you stop on the way round.

Lusion works because the site is one world you fly through, not ten slides. Halden works because it behaves like a well-edited magazine — restraint, big type, confident silence. We take the first's continuity and the second's editorial control, and we bind them with the brand: the orbit ring in the logo becomes the scroll progress ring, the planet "O" becomes the object we circle, and the stencil gaps in the wordmark become the mask through which each chapter is revealed. Nothing is decorative — every animation is a position on the orbit.

06 — Breaking the logo into a system

The mark is a toolkit, not a picture.

The Orbitvale logo is unusually rich for a graphic — a stencil wordmark with a layered swoosh. Pulled apart, it gives us the ring, the planet, the stencil mask, the data glyph and the direction arrow. That's five recurring motifs that make the whole site feel authored rather than assembled.

The swoosh sits in two layers — behind the O·R·B and in front of the V·A·L·E
◯
The ring
Becomes scroll progress — a hairline ellipse that fills as you travel the page, pinned to the edge of the viewport. Same shape, same tilt (−13°), everywhere.
O
The planet
The open "O" is a sphere. It's the object of the whole home page — the hero scene orbits it, services orbit it, the wheel is built from its ring. One object, nine chapters.
≡
The stencil slots
The gaps in the letters (dashed E, slit B, open O) are a mask language. Sections reveal through letter-shaped cut-outs; scan-lines use the same rhythm. It's the brand's texture.
Λ
The crossing slash
The crossbar-less "A" and the "V" read as a vector / velocity mark. Used as the scroll-down cue, the next-project arrow, and the play button on the showreel.
✳
The layers
The swoosh passing front-and-back is a depth rule: in the hero, satellites must occlude the ring when they pass behind it. This small correctness is what separates "3D" from "3D feeling".

08 — Rules that make it award-grade

RULE 01

One hero per viewport

Never two competing shaders or two 3D canvases visible at once. The eye needs a single subject or it reads as noise.

RULE 02

Scroll is the only timeline

One master scroll progress drives the camera. Every chapter listens to the same clock, so nothing ever gets out of sync.

RULE 03

Loud → quiet → loud

Pacing is the whole trick. Hero (loud) → statement (quiet) → services (medium) → black hole (loud) → globe (medium) → CTA (loud). Halden's editorial restraint, Lusion's spectacle.

RULE 04

Cut the canvas when it's gone

Every R3F canvas mounts on approach and unmounts when off-screen. Cap DPR at 1.5–2. This is what keeps a 9-chapter 3D page under a bearable load time.

RULE 05

Reduced motion is a first-class path

If prefers-reduced-motion is set, the story degrades to static frames + opacity fades — still beautiful, zero motion. Several components already ship this hook.

RULE 06

Sound is off by default

A tiny ambient bed with a visible mute, like Lusion. Adds an enormous amount of "premium" for the people who opt in, and costs nothing for those who don't.

RULE 07

Every frame earns its bytes

Text loads instantly; the 3D fills in behind it. No blocking spinner on a page the user can already read. That single decision is the difference between "impressive" and "usable".
